Gaussian Regression

Gaussian regression models a continuous outcome as a mean function plus normally distributed noise. The line is the expected value; the spread is how much individual observations vary around it.

The Model

For a single feature, the model says each target value is drawn from a normal distribution whose center moves linearly with the input. That makes ordinary least squares more than a curve-fitting trick: under Gaussian noise, minimizing squared error is the same direction as maximizing likelihood.

y_i \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_i,\sigma^2), \quad \mu_i=\beta_0+\beta_1x_i

Five Continuous Regression Examples

Housing

Predict sale price from location, size, and age.

Forecast delivery time from route distance and load.

Reading The Chart

The dots are sampled observations. The solid line is the fitted conditional mean. The pale band marks roughly two noise standard deviations above and below the mean, so wider bands mean less certainty about individual outcomes.

When sigma grows, the fitted line can still estimate the average trend, but individual predictions become less precise. When the slope changes, the expected value changes faster as x increases.

Use It When

  • The target is continuous: price, height, time, temperature, revenue.
  • Residuals are roughly symmetric around zero.
  • You care about both average trend and prediction uncertainty.
