1. Conditional bridge and today’s meaning

This is the second optional, authored bridge day. Continue only if yesterday’s basic preterite and your A1 foundations are usable. Completion is not an A2 certificate.

The Swedish perfect connects a past action to experience, a present result, or a time period that is still open. Its form is har + supinum (the supine).

Jag har varit i Stockholm.

I have been to Stockholm.

Experience; no finished past time is named.

Jag har köpt en biljett.

I have bought a ticket.

Completed action with a result now: I have the ticket.

Jag har arbetat mycket i dag.

I have worked a lot today.

The period “today” is still open.

2. Build the form: har + supine

Har is the finite verb and stays the same for every subject. The second verb is the supine, not the infinitive: Jag har arbetat, not Jag har arbeta.

4. Choose perfect or preterite by meaning

Perfect: connected to now / no finished timePreterite: event in a finished time
Jag har varit i Sverige.
I have been to Sweden.		Jag var i Sverige förra året.
I was in Sweden last year.
Jag har läst boken.
I have read the book.		Jag läste boken i går.
I read the book yesterday.
Vi har köpt en biljett.
We have bought a ticket.		Vi köpte biljetten i måndags.
We bought the ticket on Monday.
Safe beginner rule: use preterite with a clearly finished time such as i går, förra veckan, år 2025. Do not combine a neutral perfect exercise with i går: prefer Jag åt i går, not Jag har ätit i går.

5. Make negatives, questions and experience answers

Jag har inte ätit än.

I have not eaten yet.

inte follows finite har.

Jag har aldrig varit i Norge.

I have never been to Norway.

aldrig expresses “never.”

Har du sett filmen?

Have you seen the film?

Yes/no question: har before subject.

Vad har du gjort i dag?

What have you done today?

Question word + har + subject + supine.

Ja, jag har redan sett den.

Yes, I have already seen it.

redan means “already.”
