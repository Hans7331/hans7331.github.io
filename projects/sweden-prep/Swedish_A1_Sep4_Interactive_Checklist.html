1. Swedish alphabet — what you actually need today

You do not need to memorise the alphabet as a song. You need to recognise Swedish letters and become comfortable with the three extra letters:

LetterBeginner sound ideaExamples
Å åA rounded vowel; listen and imitate rather than forcing an English equivalent.år (year), gå (go/walk)
Ä äOften close to the vowel in English “bed”, depending on the word/accent.här (here), äta (eat)
Ö öNo exact common English equivalent; rounded lips with an “e”-like tongue position.öl (beer), köpa (buy)
Important: Swedish vowel length matters. Today, focus on listening and copying rather than mastering every pronunciation rule.

In normal speech, written de is commonly pronounced approximately like dom. For today, just recognise that spoken form.

5. The two verbs for today: är and har

vara → är

är = am / is / are

Jag är trött. — I am tired.

Hon är hemma. — She is at home.

Vi är i Finland. — We are in Finland.

ha → har

har = have / has

Jag har en bil. — I have a car.

Han har ett jobb. — He has a job.

De har tid. — They have time.

Big beginner shortcut: Swedish present-tense verbs do not change by person. So it is jag är, du är, vi är and jag har, hon har, de har.
