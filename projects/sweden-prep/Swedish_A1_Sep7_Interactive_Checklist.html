1. Learn every noun with en or ett

Swedish nouns have grammatical gender. Most are en-words and the rest are ett-words. The article is part of the word you must learn: memorise en stol, not only stol.

Indefinite singular means “a/an”
en-wordEnglishett-wordEnglish
en lägenhetan apartmentett husa house
en dörra doorett ruma room
en stola chairett köka kitchen
en sänga bedett fönstera window
en lampaa lampett borda table
There is no fully reliable shortcut for choosing en or ett. Guessing creates a habit you later have to undo; say and write the article every time you review a noun.

Jag bor i en lägenhet.

I live in an apartment.

Det finns ett kök i lägenheten.

There is a kitchen in the apartment.

Rummet har ett fönster.

The room has a window.

Det finns en lampa på bordet.

There is a lamp on the table.

2. Indefinite and definite forms

English puts the before a noun. Swedish normally attaches a definite ending to the noun. Compare en bil (“a car”) with bilen (“the car”).

The article moves to the end
IndefiniteDefiniteMeaning
en bilbilena car / the car
en bokbokena book / the book
en soffasoffana sofa / the sofa
ett hushuseta house / the house
ett rumrummeta room / the room
ett äppleäppletan apple / the apple
A useful pattern is -en/-n for many en-words and -et/-t for many ett-words. Spelling can adjust, as in rum → rummet and äpple → äpplet, so learn the definite form with the noun.

3. Basic plurals: learn the whole noun family

Swedish has several plural patterns. At A1, do not try to force every noun into one rule. Learn four useful forms together: indefinite singular, definite singular, indefinite plural and definite plural.

5. Read a short home description

Mira bor i ett hus. Huset har fyra rum: ett kök, ett vardagsrum och två sovrum. I köket finns ett bord och fyra stolar. I vardagsrummet finns en soffa. Badrummet har en dusch och ett fönster.

Mira lives in a house. It has four rooms: a kitchen, a living room and two bedrooms. The kitchen has a table and four chairs. The living room has a sofa. The bathroom has a shower and a window.

  • Where does Mira live?
  • How many rooms does the house have?
  • What is in the kitchen?
  • Which two things are in the bathroom?

6. Quick self-test

7. September 7 milestone — an eight-sentence home or room description

Describe your real, previous, ideal or imagined home/room in exactly eight original Swedish sentences. This lets everyone practise without sharing private information.

  • Say where you live or what kind of home it is.
  • Say how many rooms there are.
  • Name rooms and furniture with the correct articles.
  • Use at least two definite forms and two plurals.

Number the sentences 1–8 and check the article and noun ending in each.
