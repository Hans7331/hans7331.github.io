Core pattern: Swedish present verbs do not change with the subject: jag arbetar, du arbetar, vi arbetar, de arbetar.

Jag arbetar i Helsingfors.

I work in Helsinki.

Statement: subject + finite verb + rest.

Jag arbetar inte i dag.

I am not working today.

In a simple main clause, inte follows the finite verb.

Jag kan inte komma i dag.

I cannot come today.

With a modal, inte follows the modal.

Vill du köpa mat?

Do you want to buy food?

A yes/no question begins with the finite verb.

Use at least four different subjects. Include two negatives and two yes/no questions.

Learn a noun as a package: article + singular + useful plural. Adjectives commonly use the base form with an en-word, -t with an ett-word, and -a in plural or definite phrases.

6. One hundred distinct words — closed-book recall

This is a real 100-word test. Fill all 100 numbered boxes from memory. Each entry must be one Swedish word. A repeated word, a proper name, or another form of the same word family does not add a new recall item.
  1. Close the PDF, earlier HTML pages, notes and flashcards.
  2. Fill every box. Use the category labels as prompts, not as answers.
  3. Compare the entries with one another and replace duplicates.
  4. Only after finishing, reveal the source-based model bank. Your own valid Swedish words may differ.

7. Prepare the two-minute recording

Speak across several first-week topics so the recording tests sentence building, not one memorised paragraph. Use short, reliable sentences and keep going.

  • About you: name, origin, city and languages.
  • Family/home: two or three people or objects, with an adjective or possessive.
  • Food: what you like or need and one café request.
  • Shopping: ask a price and say how you pay.
  • Questions: include at least two questions and one negative sentence.

Do not write a full script. The goal is to build sentences while speaking.
