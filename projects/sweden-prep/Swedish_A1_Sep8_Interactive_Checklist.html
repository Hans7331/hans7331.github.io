1. Adjective agreement: en, ett and plural

An adjective changes to agree with the noun. A common pattern is the base form with an en-word, -t with an ett-word, and -a with a plural.

Core agreement patterns
Meaningen-wordett-wordPlural
bigen stor bilett stort husstora bilar / stora hus
newen ny bokett nytt jobbnya böcker / nya jobb
cleanen ren lägenhetett rent rumrena lägenheter / rena rum
expensiveen dyr stolett dyrt borddyra stolar / dyra bord
niceen trevlig granneett trevligt hemtrevliga grannar / trevliga hem

Agreement also appears after är: Bilen är stor. Huset är stort. Bilarna är stora.

Lägenheten är ren.

The apartment is clean.

Köket är rent.

The kitchen is clean.

Rummen är rena.

The rooms are clean.

Huset är stort men rummet är litet.

The house is big, but the room is small.

3. My and your: min/mitt/mina and din/ditt/dina

The possessive agrees with the thing possessed, not with the owner. Use min/din with one en-word, mitt/ditt with one ett-word, and mina/dina with any plural.

Possessive agreement
Noun typemyyour
one en-wordmin bil, min systerdin bil, din syster
one ett-wordmitt hus, mitt barnditt hus, ditt barn
pluralmina bilar, mina barndina bilar, dina barn
A possessive replaces the article: min syster, not min en syster. With an adjective, use the definite/weak adjective form: min stora bil, mitt stora hus, mina stora rum.

Min syster är ung.

My sister is young.

Mitt barn är fem år.

My child is five years old.

Mina föräldrar bor i Åbo.

My parents live in Turku.

Din lägenhet är stor.

Your apartment is big.

Ditt kök är litet.

Your kitchen is small.

Dina vänner är trevliga.

Your friends are nice.

4. Recognise the other common possessives

Useful recognition set

hans, hennes and deras do not change with the noun. vår/vårt/våra and er/ert/era do change. Today’s active production target remains min/mitt/mina and din/ditt/dina.

7. Quick self-test

8. September 8 milestone — a one-minute family and home talk

Give a one-minute talk about family and home. Real, chosen, imagined or fictional details are all acceptable. Use key words for planning, but do not read a full script in the final round.

  • Mention at least two people or relationships.
  • Describe a home or room.
  • Use min/mitt/mina or din/ditt/dina at least three times.
  • Use correct en-, ett- and plural adjective forms.

Aim for 8–12 short sentences. Correct possessive and adjective agreement before recording.
